Stop watching prices.

A plain-language guide to what this desk shows you, how it's built, the thinking behind it, and how to fold it into your own trading edge.

01 · What this is

An intermarket rotation desk

Instead of staring at the price of one ETF, this desk watches how money moves between assets — and turns that into a clear read on three things: the market regime (risk-on or risk-off), which themes are turning on or off, and where to actually focus today.

Price alone lies. The S&P at "all-time highs" tells you nothing about whether the rally is healthy or quietly rotting underneath. The relationships between assets do.

02 · The idea behind it

Why ratios, not prices

When you divide one asset by another, you strip out the broad-market tide and see who's really winning. A handful of these relationships have decades of history as early-warning gauges:

  • Stocks ÷ Bonds — are investors reaching for risk, or hiding?
  • Junk credit ÷ safe credit — is the credit market calm or stressed? Credit usually breaks before stocks do.
  • Copper ÷ Gold — does the world expect growth, or fear?
  • High-beta ÷ low-volatility — is the crowd aggressive or defensive?

This is classic intermarket analysis, in the lineage of John Murphy. The desk simply automates roughly thirty of these relationships, reads them every few minutes, and concludes for you.

Confirm the regime first. Themes are the last thing you add — and the first thing you cut.
03 · How it's built

A funnel — from weather to houses

REGIME→THEMES→FOCUS
Layer 1 · the weather

Live Signal Cards

20+ intermarket ratios, each compared to its own 20-day and 50-day moving average. Four "master gauges" carry the most weight. Everything rolls into a single weighted score and a one-line verdict at the top: risk-on, risk-off, or mixed. That's your green light for taking risk at all.

Layer 2 · which neighborhoods are open

Theme Signal Lights

Plain on/off switches for the popular thematic baskets — AI/semis, defense, speculative growth, quantum, biotech, the Mag-7 and more. Each one speaks in actions: GREEN add where leadership is, RED cut what to drop first.

Layer 3 · which houses to buy

Where to Focus — concluded for you

The payoff layer. It scans every individual stock in the universe and concludes: which side to trade, which sectors, which names, at what entry level, with what stop, and what position size for your account.

Top-down, the way rotation trading is meant to work: read the macro tide first, decide which baskets deserve capital, then pick the specific entries.

04 · The edge in coverage

It sees what a human can't

100s
every 10 min

The desk re-scans hundreds of stocks across the whole universe every 10 minutes — trend structure, relative strength, volume and volatility on each one — and re-concludes from scratch.

No human can do that. You can watch a handful of charts well; you cannot eyeball 400 names, rank them by leadership, check each against its sector and the broad market, and re-do it every ten minutes — all day, without fatigue or bias. The machine never gets tired, never falls in love with a ticker, and never forgets to check the one name that just turned.

That's the real job here: it does the brute-force, unglamorous scanning so your attention is free for the decisions that actually need a human — judgment, timing, and risk.

05 · How to read it in 30 seconds

The quick routine

  • Top verdict + score — risk-on, risk-off, or mixed? That sets your aggression for the day.
  • Master gauges — you want at least 2 of 4 bullish before taking real risk.
  • Theme lights — green = where to lean, red = where to be out.
  • Where to Focus — drop in for the specific names and levels.

Green means lean in, red means step back — and the colour is consistent everywhere on the page.

06 · How the signals are built

Layered confirmation, not one indicator

A signal is never a single line crossing another. It only earns weight when several independent things agree:

  • Trend structure — where price sits relative to its short- and longer-term averages (is it trending, and is the bigger trend backing it?).
  • Relative strength — is the name actually outperforming its own sector, and is that sector outperforming the broad market? Real leadership, not just floating up with everything.
  • Participation — is volume confirming the move, or is it hollow?
  • Volatility — measured (via ATR) so that entries, stops and position size are all derived from how much the thing actually moves, not a fixed guess.

Then everything is weighted by importance — the macro and credit master gauges count far more than a minor rotational ratio, and the strongest signals count double. So the final read is a consensus across trend, leadership, participation and risk — which is exactly what filters out the noise that traps single-indicator systems.

The exact thresholds and weightings are the proprietary part. The principle is simple and deliberate: nothing gets a green light on one piece of evidence alone.

07 · For every trader

Bring your own edge — it stacks

This desk is the context layer, not a replacement for your own method. It answers "is the environment right, and where?" — it doesn't pretend to nail the exact tick. That's where you come in:

  • Use it as a filter. Only take your long setups when the regime is green and the theme light is green. Skip the same setup in a red regime — that one rule removes a lot of bad trades.
  • Time the entry with your own tools. Use your momentum / oscillator / price-action triggers to time the entry once the desk says the direction and environment are favourable.
  • Use it for confirmation. If your indicator flags a long and the desk's regime + theme + relative-strength all agree, that's high conviction. If they disagree — size down, or pass.
  • Use it to manage risk. The ATR-based stops and sizing are a sane default you can adopt or adapt.
The desk tells you whether and where. Your edge tells you exactly when. They stack.
08 · Honest limits

What it is not

  • It works off closing-price relationships and ~15-minute-delayed data — a regime and rotation tool, not a scalping feed.
  • All signals are inherently a little lagging — that's the price of robustness. It waits for confirmation instead of guessing tops and bottoms.
  • It is an educational framework, not personalised investment advice. Always layer it on top of your own risk management.
